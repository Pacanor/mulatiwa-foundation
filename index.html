Why Mulatiwa

Change takes more than one intervention.

We work in four connected areas: youth leadership and education; community resilience and environment; protection, equity and wellbeing; and evidence and advocacy.

We manage grants and the community side of research and development programmes in Zambia. While our team consists of competent and seasoned researchers, Mulatiwa Foundation does hold and account for funds on behalf of researchers and other organisations, handle community entry and consent, safeguard participants, and turn findings into something communities and young people can use.

Our work sits with various Zambian research institutions, and we collaborate with other stakeholders within and outside of Zambia.

Our work

Four connected paths
to lasting impact.

Four connected areas of work, carried with communities, researchers, ministries and funders.

01

Youth & education

Leadership from age 10 to 19, entrepreneurial skills, vocational and employment pathways, and financial literacy for girls and young women, one child at a time.

  • Youth leadership
  • Vocational skills
  • Financial literacy
02

Climate & community

Locally owned community projects, climate sensitisation, and work on how climate stress affects the health and mental health of adolescents.

  • Community projects
  • Climate and health
  • Adolescent wellbeing
03

Protection & wellbeing

Tackling abuse, marginalisation and the norms that keep girls out of school, and supporting healthy ageing and care for elderly women.

  • Safe communities
  • Healthy ageing
  • Equity in education
04

Evidence & advocacy

Impact assessment of our programmes, policy engagement, and mentoring between emerging and established leaders.

  • Impact assessment
  • Policy engagement
  • Leadership mentoring
Meet Mulatiwa Academy ↓
12 to 19 planned learner age range

Flagship initiative

A bold vision for a girls’ leadership school that combines academic excellence with character, confidence and care.

The planned secondary boarding school opens in 2028 with 100 learners aged 12 to 19, rising to 360 at full capacity, with up to 40% of places on full or partial scholarships. It will blend a STEM-forward, Cambridge aligned curriculum with mentorship, leadership, integrity, public speaking, service learning, wellbeing support and global exposure.
